## Changed
- `resources/upgrades/net_width.tres`: curve_b 0.22→0.16, curve_c 0.03→0.045, value_cap 8.06→10.3
- `resources/upgrades/net_range.tres`: curve_b 0.95→0.7, curve_c 0.09→0.13, value_cap 39.6→47.9
- `resources/upgrades/reel.tres`: curve_b 0.7→0.5, curve_c 0.06→0.075, level_cap 17→20, value_cap 30→42.4
- `resources/upgrades/net_hold.tres`: curve_b 1→0.5, curve_c 0.25→0.2, level_cap 18→22, value_cap 100→110
- `resources/upgrades/boat_speed.tres`: price_mult 1.42→1.35, curve_c 0→0.025, level_cap 16→18, value_cap 25→35.7
- `resources/upgrades/cargo.tres`: price_mult 1.42→1.30, curve_c 0→0.05, level_cap 24→28, value_cap 100→157
- `resources/upgrades/fleet.tres`: price_mult 2.6→2.0, level_cap 2→4
- `scripts/lake.gd`: `MAX_BOATS` 3→5 (matches fleet's new level_cap of 4 extra hulls)
- New: `scripts/upgrade_track.gd` (`UpgradeTrack` resource class) and `resources/upgrades/*.tres`
  (8 files, `net_strength.tres` included with its values unchanged) replace the hardcoded
  `PRICES` / `MAX_LEVELS` / formula constants for these 8 tracks, so the next pass on any of
  them is an Inspector edit, not a `lake.gd` edit. `skimmer` was left on the old const system —
  its payoff is a chance curve, not this price/value shape.

## Hypothesis
Reported after a full playthrough: near-max power reached early, then the back ~2/3 of the
run had almost nothing left to buy — and the yard held "thousands" of pieces the whole game,
because the boat side (linear `boat_speed`/`cargo`, `fleet` capped at 2 extra hulls behind a
2.6x price curve) could never keep up with the net side (four stats — radius, range, reel,
hold — each accelerating and all improving at once).

Three changes, matching what was reported:

1. **Early upgrades a little weaker** — lowered the linear (`curve_b`) term on the net tracks,
   so the first several levels give less of a jump, slowing how fast net power (and the income
   it generates) snowballs in the opening stretch.
2. **Later upgrades stronger and cheaper** — raised the quadratic (`curve_c`) term and
   `value_cap` on the same net tracks so late levels keep paying off instead of flatlining;
   gave `reel` and `net_hold` more levels (17→20, 18→22) so there's more to buy deeper into a
   run; cut `cargo` and `boat_speed`'s price multiplier (1.42→1.30/1.35) so the late levels are
   actually reachable, not a wall.
3. **Boats fix the pile-up** — `cargo`'s ceiling raised 100→157 with a new mild quadratic ramp,
   `fleet`'s price cut 2.6x→2.0x, and `MAX_BOATS` raised 3→5 (confirmed safe: boats are built
   dynamically in `_add_boat()`, no fixed-scene limit) — real headroom to out-haul the net,
   instead of the old hard 3-boat ceiling the code assumed (wrongly, per this playtest) was
   already more than the yard could fill.

`net_strength` and `skimmer` untouched — not part of what was reported.

These are reasoned from the actual formulas and total costs-to-max, not simulated against real
play seconds (several unknown timings — cast/haul animation lengths — aren't in the numbers
read for this pass), so treat the exact magnitudes as a first hypothesis, not a verified fix.

## Observed
Played on a fresh save. Reported: upgrades now max out very fast — pointing specifically at
how much selling pays, not at the upgrade curves from the section above.

## Decision
Keep the upgrade-curve changes above (not implicated — pile-up wasn't re-reported). Sell price
is a new, separate problem: see the follow-up below.

## Follow-up (same day) — sell price too high

### Changed
- New: `scripts/economy_config.gd` (`EconomyConfig` resource class) and `resources/economy.tres`,
  replacing the `BIRD_BONUS` / `PIECE_BASE_PAY` / `PIECE_FILTH_PAY` constants in `lake.gd`.
- `piece_base_pay`: 4.0 → 2.5
- `piece_filth_pay`: 9.0 → 6.0
- `bird_bonus`: unchanged at 26.0 — not implicated, left alone

### Hypothesis
On a fresh save, upgrades maxed out very fast — called out specifically as a money-from-selling
problem, not an upgrade-curve problem (this pass's earlier section already made net/boat curves
harder to outrun; that part wasn't re-reported as broken).

Nothing about this pass's earlier changes touches sell price. But cargo and fleet got
substantially cheaper and boat throughput went up (that was the point, to fix the yard
pile-up) — so, for the same per-piece pay as before, a lot more pieces now reach a merchant
per minute than they used to. Total income throughput went up even though nothing about "what
one piece pays" changed. Cutting the per-piece pay ~30-35% is a direct countermeasure sized to
roughly match that throughput increase.

Flat/filth split kept proportionate (both cut by a comparable amount) rather than favoring one
end of the run over the other, since nothing so far points at early vs. late specifically —
just "overall, too much."

### Observed
Not yet played on the new numbers.

### Decision
Pending next playthrough. If upgrades still race ahead, the next lever is probably
`piece_filth_pay` specifically (it's what makes late, heavy pieces worth so much) rather than
cutting `piece_base_pay` further — cutting the flat fee too hard risks making the early game
feel like it doesn't pay at all, which is a different complaint than the one on record.

## Follow-up 2 (same day) — net_strength as a strategic gate, wall clock's tier

### Changed
- `resources/upgrades/net_strength.tres`: price_base 22→80, price_mult 1.52→2.3 (level_cap
  stays 4 — nothing to gate past tier 4)
- `resources/trash/wall_clock.tres` ↔ `resources/trash/cooking_pot.tres`: swapped `tier`,
  `pollution`, and `haul_cost` only (each keeps its own size/lightness/color/art) — wall clock
  is now tier 2 (pollution 3.2, haul_cost 2.6), cooking pot is tier 4 (pollution 4.5, haul_cost
  4.2)

### Hypothesis
1. **net_strength**: only 4 levels and, untouched since the first pass, still the cheapest
   track in the game by a wide margin (~183 sludge to fully max, against 5,000-10,000+ for
   most other tracks) — requested directly: make it a real strategic choice ("where do I focus
   while cleaning") rather than something bought on the way to something else. A steep price
   (2.3x/level, ~1,660 total to max) makes each of the 4 levels a real commitment, so which
   tier of material is reachable becomes a deliberate call, not a formality.
2. **Wall clock tier**: called out directly — a wall clock is not plausibly the heaviest thing
   in the lake, but the data had it at tier 4 (net_strength's max, alongside the urn) while a
   cooking pot — the more plausible "heavy" object — sat at tier 2. Swapped their gameplay
   numbers (tier/pollution/haul_cost) without touching either one's art, size, or how it sits
   in the heap, since those describe the object itself, not the tier slot it occupies. Total
   distribution across the 16 pieces is unchanged, so this doesn't reopen the pacing math from
   the first pass — just reassigns which named piece sits at which rung.

### Observed
Not yet played — before a playtest came back, the direction was pushed further (see follow-up
3): level_cap 4 stayed, but the price on `net_strength` went up again.

### Decision
Superseded by follow-up 3 below on `net_strength`'s price. The wall-clock/cooking-pot swap
stands as-is pending playtest.

## Follow-up 3 (same day) — net_strength pushed to mid/late game

### Changed
- `resources/upgrades/net_strength.tres`: price_base 80→150, price_mult 2.3→6.0 (level_cap
  stays 4)

### Hypothesis
Follow-up 2's 2.3x wasn't steep enough — asked for net_strength to max at mid-to-end game, not
just "cost more." With only 4 levels, price_mult is the one lever that controls *when* the
last level lands, so it took a large jump: cumulative cost is now 150 (level 1) → 1,050 (level
2) → 6,450 (level 3) → 38,850 (level 4, tier 4 — the last 32,400 of that in one purchase). That
spread puts the first bump early, the middle two roughly mid-game, and the last one — full net
power — squarely late, alongside or after the point where the big-ticket boat/net_hold
purchases start landing.

Sized off this session's own numbers (comparable to `net_width`'s pre-session cost-to-max of
~9,700 and above it, on the reasoning that net_strength is now the deliberately special,
expensive track), not off measured play time — flag it as the roughest-sized number in
today's pass.

### Observed
Not yet played.

### Decision
Pending next playthrough. If tier 4 (the 32,400 jump) is never reached in a normal run, ease
`price_mult` down (6.0 → ~4.5) before touching `price_base`. If it's still reached too early,
raise `price_mult` further rather than `price_base` — the base only controls the first,
supposed-to-stay-cheap level.

## Follow-up 4 (2026-09-06) — net_width / net_hold: the quadratic-area mechanism

### Changed
- `resources/upgrades/net_width.tres`: price_mult 1.52 → 1.6 (curve/value fields untouched from
  this morning's earlier pass)
- `resources/upgrades/net_hold.tres`: price_mult 1.52 → 1.55 (curve/value fields untouched)

### Hypothesis
Reported after further play: still too powerful too fast, specifically from net width and
net hold — despite this morning's curve_b/curve_c softening on all four net tracks.

Traced the actual mechanism in `net.gd`/`lake_grid.gd` before touching any more numbers
(previous passes today reasoned from formulas alone; this one checked the code they act on).
Two things width/hold have that net_range and reel don't:

1. **`net_width`'s value becomes a radius, and catchable tiles are tested as
   `dx² + dy² ≤ radius²`** (a circle-area check) — so a curve that looks linear-ish in level
   (`curve_a + curve_b*L + curve_c*L²`) produces **quadratic growth in the actual money-per-
   cast power** (swept tile area), not the roughly-linear growth the curve shape suggests.
   Lowering `curve_b` this morning slowed the *value* number, but the felt power is that
   value squared — so the real slowdown was smaller than intended.
2. **`net_hold` is a hard additive cap on pieces-per-cast, no formula multiplies it with
   width** — but once width's swept area exceeds hold, hold becomes the binding ceiling. That
   means buying whichever of the two is currently NOT the bottleneck banks free value, and
   buying the other one then unlocks it all at once — an emergent compounding return neither
   track's own (identical: price_base 22, price_mult 1.52) cost curve accounts for.

Lever chosen: `price_mult` specifically (not another curve_b/curve_c cut, to avoid re-opening
this morning's already-made value-curve decision) — slows how fast the pair gets bought
without changing the power ceiling already tuned today. Sized asymmetrically because
`net_hold`'s 22-level cap makes it far more sensitive to the same multiplier than
`net_width`'s 13: a flat 1.52→1.6 for both would have roughly tripled hold's cost-to-max.
Computed with `tools/balance_calc.py`, not by hand:
- `net_width` total cost to max: 9,738.8 → 16,476.5 (+69%)
- `net_hold` total cost to max: 423,579.5 → 615,643.3 (+45%)
- Early levels (1–5) barely move for either track — the slowdown is backloaded, aimed at
  "too fast to reach strong," not "too strong at the start."

### Observed
Not yet played — this is today's newest, most-targeted hypothesis on this specific
complaint; needs its own playtest before anything stacks on top of it.

### Decision
Pending playtest. If still too fast, the next lever is probably pushing `price_mult` further
on these same two fields (the mechanism, not the specific numbers, is what's been verified
here) rather than touching curve_a/b/c again, since that would re-litigate this morning's
value-curve pass on top of an unverified one.

## Open item (not acted on) — "money made should exactly match total upgrade costs"

Requested: total achievable income over a full playthrough should land exactly on total
cost-to-max across every track, no leftover, nothing unreachable.

Not attempted this pass — flagging why rather than guessing at it:

1. **"Exactly," literally, isn't a sound target.** Any player who misses pieces, plays
   imperfectly, or skips birds/charms earns less than the theoretical maximum — an "exact"
   design has no slack for that and goes negative (something unreachable) for the first
   player who isn't perfect. A **band** (e.g. total income lands within ~90–110% of total
   upgrade cost for a normal-skill full clear) is achievable; an exact equality isn't a
   meaningful target for a game with player-skill variance.
2. **Computing the achievable-income side requires a fact this pass didn't gather**: the
   actual per-piece payout and rubbish-spawn distribution live in `lake.gd` (not read in
   detail this pass — it's the 100KB file). Total cost-to-max across every current track is
   known (943,453.4 after this pass's two changes, computed via `balance_calc.py`), but total
   achievable income isn't, without reading that logic.

This is a separate, sizable hypothesis from the width/hold one above — per this session's own
new rule, it shouldn't stack on top of an unverified change in the same pass. Next step, if
wanted: extend `tools/balance_calc.py` (or a new script) to also model total achievable
income from `lake.gd`'s actual formulas, so "does total income roughly match total upgrade
cost" becomes a real, checkable number instead of a guess — as a separate pass, after this
one's been played.
